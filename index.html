Riverside Distance Lab

A small riverside town with walls, buildings and two bridges. Eight occupants walk between places. Pick any two locations or occupants and compare three ways of measuring how far apart they are.

Straight line City block Walking path

Click an occupant (circle), a place (square) or any open ground. A selected occupant is tracked as they walk, so the distances update live. One cell is 10 m.

Close by one metric, far by another

    How each distance is measured, and why it is a metric
    Straight line (Euclidean)

    d = √(Δx² + Δy²). This is distance as the crow flies, which ignores walls and water. It is a metric: never negative, zero only for the same cell, symmetric, and the straight segment is the shortest curve, so the triangle inequality holds.

    City block (Manhattan)

    d = |Δx| + |Δy|. You may only move along the grid axes, like streets. Each coordinate term is a metric on its own, and a sum of metrics is a metric. It is always between 1× and √2× the straight line.

    Walking path (shortest route, bonus)

    The shortest route through walkable cells, 8 directions, with diagonals costing √2 and no corner cutting. It is computed with Dijkstra’s algorithm. Every step is reversible, so it is symmetric. Joining a route A→C to a route C→B gives a route A→B, so the triangle inequality holds. It is never shorter than the straight line.
